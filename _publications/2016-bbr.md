excerpt: 'This study was part of a line of research that used survey, behavioral, and eye movement measures to examine biological bases of political attitudes and behavior. Here, we examined how political ideology influences emotional memory. Participants studied positive, negative, and neutral scenes, then completed a memory test. Results showed that negative scenes were more likely to be remembered than positive ones, but only among political conservatives. The strength of this negativity bias increased with conservatism and explained 45% of variance in emotional memory. These findings suggest that ideological differences in emotion processing extend to both memory and attention.'
